# Weapon and Character Asset Evaluation

Status: Sten Mk II asset selected and integrated as the first-person weapon. Real MakeHuman-derived hands are fitted as a current game candidate; the full human character presentation remains under art review. Verified 25 September 2026. This is a fictional European town in 1944; neither player nor guard faction is fixed by the weapon choice.

## Selected Sten Mk II

- **Creator and source:** Lotnik, [Sten mk2 on OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/sten-mk2), published 16 May 2016. The public `sten.7z` was downloaded without an account on 25 September 2026. Original archive SHA-256: `d989a973f4a57e096d3bdc47649ec191ac1e4352b1fbc13f1acfa8cbcc04609e`.
- **Rights:** The author's page labels the work [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). The runtime package must include `assets/vendor/weapon_visual/sten_mk2/ATTRIBUTION.txt` with creator, title, source, license link and changes. This is a free attribution license, not CC0. No account or paid resource was required.
- **Original files:** `source/sten_0.7z` and `source/original/sten_low.fbx`, `sten_albedo.png`, `sten_metalness.tga`, `sten_normals.jpg`, `sten_occlusion.png`. All original texture maps are 2048×2048. The FBX is a single UV-mapped mesh with 3,655 polygons, no rig or animations and no separately named magazine. These files stay out of game imports via `source/.gdignore` and out of the exported PCK.
- **Adaptation:** `source/sten_mk2_working.blend` contains the isolated Blender scene with source mesh, a split `StenBody` and independently movable `StenMagazine`. A fresh Blender 5.2.2 subprocess reopened it as an ordinary project with that scene active, three expected mesh objects and all four image paths resolving relative to the `.blend` (`//original/` and `//converted/`); the original maps remain unchanged. The game model is scaled by 0.06 to about 0.746 m long. The magazine was separated across the source model's local X=1.235 plane and is about 0.155 m wide. `tools/art/weapon_texture_pack.py` converts Unity smoothness alpha into glTF roughness and packs occlusion/roughness/metallic as R/G/B. `tools/art/weapon_glb_finalize.py` attaches the occlusion channel after Blender export. The normal map's green channel was left unchanged in this conversion, and the rendered material was reviewed with that setting. The GLB embeds albedo, normal and packed ORM images.
- **Runtime proof:** `runtime/sten_mk2.glb` SHA-256 `2fd0c05c799ead1a6ccd05ec4cba82417a5c031d1aa16ab2e15020840dbc1235`; its glTF JSON contains exactly `StenBody` and `StenMagazine` mesh nodes and no town architecture. Godot 4.7.2 imports both nodes with albedo, metal, roughness, normal and ambient-occlusion textures. Godot extracts the three embedded maps into `runtime/sten_mk2_sten_albedo.png`, `sten_mk2_sten_normals.jpg` and `sten_mk2_sten_orm.png`; those authored import results and their `.import` settings are retained beside the GLB for stable package builds. The original raw FBX did not preserve all texture links in Godot, which is why the adapted GLB is the runtime copy. A Blender viewport inspection image is in `previews/sten_live_viewport.png`; `previews/.gdignore` keeps that evidence out of the game import.

The current game uses this Sten in hip fire and aim poses, with a separately animated magazine and visual recoil. A rendered input-driven review passed firing, reload at 30% and 70%, pause/resume, and sustained crouch/stand. The support hand follows the magazine in its local transform during reload; a near-wall input-driven check showed the weapon lowering out of the wall. See [hip](media/sten_first_person_hip.png), [aim](media/sten_first_person_ads.png), and [reload](media/sten_first_person_reload.png) screenshots. These are functional and visual review evidence, not a human full-mission playtest or final art acceptance. The Sten visual does not change the existing 30-round magazine, 1.9-second reload or ballistics.

## Human Character and Hands

The [MakeHuman system asset pack](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/makehuman_system_assets.html) lists CC0 skins, body proxies and clothing, with anonymous downloads available. Its body and skin supply the current first-person hands: the 53-bone source rig was posed with curled fingers, then each hand was extracted with its UV skin and packed texture. `assets/player/hands/PROVENANCE.txt` records source, rights and changes; `source/grip_hands_working.blend` is a standalone editable two-hand file that reopened in a fresh Blender process with both textures packed. The game uses the two hand GLBs and generated cloth sleeves/cuffs. A rigged full-body contact prototype is under separate review and has not yet established a final character silhouette or animation. Neither the bare hands nor the sleeve color assigns a faction or historical uniform.

A [rigged low-poly WW2 soldier by nisu](https://opengameart.org/content/rigged-lowpoly-ww2-soldier) was examined only in ignored `reference/asset_candidates/`. It has a 49-bone skeleton, but no supplied animations and an overt German uniform/low-poly appearance. It is not integrated or proposed as the guard's final appearance. A Sketchfab MP40 listing has CC BY metadata but its original file and registration access were not verified; it was not acquired and cannot replace the confirmed SMG direction by implication. Paid or account-gated candidates were not used.
