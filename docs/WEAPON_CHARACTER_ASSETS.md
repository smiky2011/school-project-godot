# Weapon and Character Asset Evaluation

Status: On 26 September 2026 the user selected the StG 44 silhouette and authorized downloading and integrating Max Ilin / ELIZION's free original asset. Its adapted GLB is now the player's first-person weapon and has passed rendered hip, sight, tactical-magazine and empty-reload reviews. Guards still use the Sten. Real MakeHuman-derived hands are fitted as a current game candidate; the full human character presentation remains under art review. This is a fictional European town in 1944; neither player nor guard faction is fixed by the weapon choice.

## Selected StG 44 Direction and Integrated Asset

The user selected the StG 44 silhouette after reviewing period rifles. [Max Ilin / ELIZION's StG 44](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/stg-44-sturmgewehr-fa37bef729e141a6a29bb022a3e0be41) is free to download under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), with about 40,000 triangles and a worn PBR look. The user authorized the official original download and adaptation on 26 September 2026. The [artist's portfolio](https://elizion.artstation.com/projects/aYYBaL) documents the model. The official archive SHA-256 is `49dc74dc8f5356b7fd596619d9e3c14c03d729b39741d82e12c7cf13958ee3b3`; a local ignored copy and unchanged extracted source maps remain under `assets/vendor/weapon_visual/stg44/source/`. The package [attribution](../assets/vendor/weapon_visual/stg44/ATTRIBUTION.txt) and [source manifest](../assets/vendor/weapon_visual/stg44/source/SOURCE_MANIFEST.txt) record rights, download date, changes and rebuild steps.

The original FBX already has 13 named meshes, including an independent bottom `Magazine` and `Body_Bolt`. It faces +Y in Blender, which exports to Godot's -Z forward without a runtime yaw. The original mesh length is approximately 0.98 m. `tools/art/stg44_prepare_textures.py` creates 1024-pixel albedo JPG, lossless normal PNG and packed AO/roughness/metallic PNG for each of its five material sets. `tools/art/stg44_build_blender.py` creates the isolated `source/stg44_working.blend` and `runtime/stg44.glb`; `tools/art/stg44_finalize_glb.py` attaches the AO channel. A fresh Blender 5.2.2 subprocess reopened the working file and verified all 15 texture paths as repository-relative `//converted/...` paths. The GLB includes the original 13 mesh nodes and five PBR materials; Godot 4.7.2 imported it and its embedded textures without source parsing errors.

The player viewmodel uses the imported receiver, stock, barrel, magazine and bolt. The muzzle is near `(0, 0.1415, -0.464)`, measured from the front bore geometry, the ejection port near `(0.026, 0.137, -0.015)`, the trigger grip near `(0, 0.09, 0.13)`, and the support grip near `(-0.04, 0.065, -0.18)` in Godot local space. The support hand moves to the bottom magazine for reload; the magazine draws down and returns, and the separate bolt moves during an empty reload. The HUD displays `STG 44`. The same 30-round magazine, unlimited reserve, firing interval, damage and provisional handling/reload values remain. The input-driven Metal review captured hip, ADS, firing, a tactical reload at 32% and 71%, pause and crouch; it passed without script failures. A focused rendered probe then captured a forced empty reload at 36%, 70% and 87%; the bolt moved about 4.4 cm rearward, and the magazine refilled to 30 rounds. The last sight pass uses the source's real front post tip (height 0.1998 m, separate from its 0.2086 m protective hood) and rear notch side tops (0.2011 m). A calculated 0.003-radian pitch puts those points on the same sight line. The projected post tip `(640.3, 359.3)` and rear notch top `(641.2, 357.5)` were both within three pixels of the 1280 × 720 screen center `(640, 360)`, with the wooden butt behind the camera. A final single-shot probe checked that the muzzle FX marker coincided with the measured bore within 1 mm, the muzzle flash was active, and a shell appeared in the rendered frame. See [hip](media/stg44_first_person_hip.png), [ADS](media/stg44_first_person_ads.png), [shot](media/stg44_muzzle.png), [magazine withdrawn](media/stg44_empty_reload_magazine.png) and [bolt pulled](media/stg44_empty_reload_bolt.png). These are controlled rendered checks, not a human full-mission or final-art acceptance. The [exact-PCK QA](QA_REPORT.md) loaded the StG 44 parts and checked a controlled near-wall blocker: the tucked muzzle and firing flash stayed behind its face. Natural wall approach and human presentation review remain open.

## Selected Sten Mk II

- **Creator and source:** Lotnik, [Sten mk2 on OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/sten-mk2), published 16 May 2016. The public `sten.7z` was downloaded without an account on 25 September 2026. Original archive SHA-256: `d989a973f4a57e096d3bdc47649ec191ac1e4352b1fbc13f1acfa8cbcc04609e`.
- **Rights:** The author's page labels the work [CC BY 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/). The runtime package must include `assets/vendor/weapon_visual/sten_mk2/ATTRIBUTION.txt` with creator, title, source, license link and changes. This is a free attribution license, not CC0. No account or paid resource was required.
- **Original files:** `source/sten_0.7z` and `source/original/sten_low.fbx`, `sten_albedo.png`, `sten_metalness.tga`, `sten_normals.jpg`, `sten_occlusion.png`. All original texture maps are 2048×2048. The FBX is a single UV-mapped mesh with 3,655 polygons, no rig or animations and no separately named magazine. These files stay out of game imports via `source/.gdignore` and out of the exported PCK.
- **Adaptation:** `source/sten_mk2_working.blend` contains the isolated Blender scene with source mesh, a split `StenBody` and independently movable `StenMagazine`. A fresh Blender 5.2.2 subprocess reopened it as an ordinary project with that scene active, three expected mesh objects and all four image paths resolving relative to the `.blend` (`//original/` and `//converted/`); the original maps remain unchanged. The game model is scaled by 0.06 to about 0.746 m long. The magazine was separated across the source model's local X=1.235 plane and is about 0.155 m wide. `tools/art/weapon_texture_pack.py` converts Unity smoothness alpha into glTF roughness and packs occlusion/roughness/metallic as R/G/B. `tools/art/weapon_glb_finalize.py` attaches the occlusion channel after Blender export. The normal map's green channel was left unchanged in this conversion, and the rendered material was reviewed with that setting. The GLB embeds albedo, normal and packed ORM images.
- **Runtime proof:** `runtime/sten_mk2.glb` SHA-256 `2fd0c05c799ead1a6ccd05ec4cba82417a5c031d1aa16ab2e15020840dbc1235`; its glTF JSON contains exactly `StenBody` and `StenMagazine` mesh nodes and no town architecture. Godot 4.7.2 imports both nodes with albedo, metal, roughness, normal and ambient-occlusion textures. Godot extracts the three embedded maps into `runtime/sten_mk2_sten_albedo.png`, `sten_mk2_sten_normals.jpg` and `sten_mk2_sten_orm.png`; those authored import results and their `.import` settings are retained beside the GLB for stable package builds. The original raw FBX did not preserve all texture links in Godot, which is why the adapted GLB is the runtime copy. A Blender viewport inspection image is in `previews/sten_live_viewport.png`; `previews/.gdignore` keeps that evidence out of the game import.

The previous player viewmodel used this Sten in hip fire and aim poses, with a separately animated magazine and visual recoil. A rendered input-driven review passed firing, reload at 30% and 70%, pause/resume, and sustained crouch/stand. A near-wall input-driven check showed the weapon lowering out of the wall. See [hip](media/sten_first_person_hip.png), [aim](media/sten_first_person_ads.png), and [reload](media/sten_first_person_reload.png) screenshots. These are historical functional and visual evidence, not acceptance of the new StG 44 or a human full-mission playtest. Guards retain this Sten visual.

## Human Character and Hands

The [MakeHuman system asset pack](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/makehuman_system_assets.html) lists CC0 skins, body proxies and clothing, with anonymous downloads available. Its body and skin supply the current first-person hands: the 53-bone source rig was posed with curled fingers, then each hand was extracted with its UV skin and packed texture. `assets/player/hands/PROVENANCE.txt` records source, rights and changes; `source/grip_hands_working.blend` is a standalone editable two-hand file that reopened in a fresh Blender process with both textures packed. The game uses the two hand GLBs and generated cloth sleeves/cuffs. A rigged full-body contact prototype is under separate review and has not yet established a final character silhouette or animation. Neither the bare hands nor the sleeve color assigns a faction or historical uniform.

A [rigged low-poly WW2 soldier by nisu](https://opengameart.org/content/rigged-lowpoly-ww2-soldier) was examined only in ignored `reference/asset_candidates/`. It has a 49-bone skeleton, but no supplied animations and an overt German uniform/low-poly appearance. It is not integrated or proposed as the guard's final appearance. A Sketchfab MP40 listing has CC BY metadata but its original file and registration access were not verified; it was not acquired and cannot replace the confirmed SMG direction by implication. Paid or account-gated candidates were not used.
