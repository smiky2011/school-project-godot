# Player body and shadow presentation

The player has a world-space, rigged body that casts an anatomical shadow without appearing in the first-person camera. `main.gd` attaches `PlayerBodyPresentation` to the real `CharacterBody3D`. Every mesh in that presentation uses Godot's `SHADOWS_ONLY` mode. The camera-bound gun, hands and sleeves have shadow casting disabled, so they cannot project a detached first-person model onto the street.

The player body uses an independently exported version of the project's existing CC0 MakeHuman field character. The original guard model and animation remain unchanged. `source/player_shadow_locomotion.blend` is the editable source and `runtime/player_shadow_animated.glb` is the game asset. `tools/art/build_player_shadow_body.py` rebuilds both from the retained guard source with Blender 5.2.2. It adds a bent-knee, lowered-pelvis crouch and separate forward, backward and lateral gait clips. The runtime silhouette omits tiny eye and eyelash meshes and all color/normal textures; the editable source retains the original materials. It is a shadow-only reuse of the existing character, not a visible player outfit or a new licensed model. The original CC0 asset sources and acquisition date are recorded in [Character production](CHARACTER_PRODUCTION.md) and [`PROVENANCE.txt`](../assets/vendor/character_visual/makehuman/PROVENANCE.txt).

The presentation reads the player's actual grounded velocity after movement. Stationary turning and airborne movement settle to idle. Forward, backward and sideways movement choose separate authored leg clips; diagonal movement uses the dominant direction. Walking speed follows the ground speed, while crouch uses a shorter gait. The shadow follows the player's position and yaw because it is a child of the player actor. The crouched pelvis and knees lower the silhouette while feet remain near the ground. The stand/crouch transition blends over 0.18 seconds without changing the collision capsule, camera, weapon ballistics or AI.

The separate world-weapon shadow uses the imported StG 44 model. Its trigger grip point is offset to the rig's right-hand `BoneAttachment3D`; the weapon therefore follows the hand through the crouched spine pose rather than receiving a guessed vertical adjustment. The editable rifle source and its rights are recorded in the weapon's own attribution file. An authored upper-body aim/reload animation and precise camera-pitch alignment remain open. The current aim shadow retains the low-ready grip instead of moving the gun away from the shadow hands.

## Verification

Run `Godot --path . --script res://tests/rendered_player_shadow_review.gd` with a graphics display. The Metal fixture passed on 26 September 2026 with the integrated StG model. It captured idle, forward walk, backward walk, left/right strafe, crouch, crouched strafe and aim shadows plus a downward-looking first-person view under the town's actual sun. The [walking](media/player_shadow_walk.png) and [crouched](media/player_shadow_crouch.png) frames are saved from that same passing run. It also checked that turning in place, pushing into a wall and jumping do not cycle a walking pose. Standing ankle/head heights were 0.047/1.560 m and crouched heights were -0.014/1.183 m in this street, while the knee angle changed from 6.9 to 72.8 degrees. The crouched strafe ankle was -0.003 m. The world weapon mount stayed exactly at the right-hand bone position in both sampled poses. The fixture checked that world meshes only cast shadows and camera-bound gun/hands/sleeves do not. `build/qa/player_shadow_*.png` is ignored test output. This controlled camera inspection is not a human visual acceptance or a full mission playthrough; the upper-body aim/reload and camera-pitch shadow poses remain the limitation described above.
